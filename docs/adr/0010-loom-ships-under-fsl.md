# Loom ships under FSL-1.1-ALv2 (Fair Source, Apache-2.0 after two years)

Loom is licensed under the Functional Source License 1.1 with the Apache-2.0 future grant (canonical abbreviation FSL-1.1-ALv2; formerly published as FSL-1.1-Apache-2.0): businesses may self-host Loom to power their own products, but may not offer a commercialized competing context engine; each release automatically converts to Apache-2.0 on its second anniversary. The Apache future grant was chosen over the MIT variant for its patent grant, which matters for a server product enterprises self-host. FSL is not OSI open source, so public wording must say "Fair Source" or "source-available", never "open source". This does not constrain runtime dependencies: GPLv3/AGPL services (Neo4j Community, potential future engines) run as separate processes and create no derivation.

Rejected: Apache-2.0 (maximum adoption but no protection against managed-service freeriders — the HydraDB-shaped risk this clause exists for) and AGPL-3.0 (freerider deterrence via copyleft, but enterprise procurement friction against the "self-hostable for your product" adoption path, and no automatic return to permissive terms).
